01 — The short version

Six changes, in the order they should be built

01
Menu button moves to the left

A one-line change with an outsized effect: the control that opens navigation should sit where navigation lives. Also makes it thumb-reachable on a left-held tablet.

02
Group the 21 nav links

An owner currently sees twenty-one undifferentiated links in one column. Six labelled groups turn a list into a map.

03
One page header component

Every screen currently assembles its own title, subtitle, help affordance and action row by hand, with different markup each time. One component, one rhythm.

04
Status becomes a component

Flags, pills, ratio banners and the offline strip all say "state" in slightly different visual languages. Consolidate into one Status primitive with four tones.

05
Forms get a two-column shell

The incident form is the longest thing anyone fills in under pressure. Explanation on the left, fields on the right, a sticky action bar at the bottom.

06
Empty and first-run states

A new centre's first hour is currently a series of blank pages. Each list screen gets an empty state that names the next action.

Nothing here touches packages/core/src/tokens.ts. The palette is generated, its contrast ratios are recomputed at build time and tokens:check already guards it. Changing colour would be a regression, not a redesign — the product does not look unfinished because of its colours, it looks unfinished because of its structure.

02 — The menu button

Top right to top left

The drawer opens from the left edge. The control that opens it currently sits at the opposite corner, so the tap and the result happen at two different ends of the screen. Moving it removes that gap and puts it under the thumb of a hand holding a tablet at the door.

03 — Navigation

Twenty-one links, no grouping

The layout renders every capability-filtered link into one flat <nav>. For an educator that is nine links and workable. For an owner it is twenty-one, and the daily screens sit visually equal to the ones opened twice a year. The order in the source is already thoughtful — the code comments argue each adjacency. Making that reasoning visible is most of the fix.

{{ group.label }}
{{ item }}

Account, Notifications and Help move to the rail footer, beside Switch centre and Sign out. They are about the person, not the centre, and they are the only three links a parent and an owner share identically.

A group renders only when at least one of its links survives the capability filter, so an educator sees three headings and a parent sees none — the flat list is still correct for them.

04 — Page header

One component instead of four spellings

Attendance wraps its title in .page-head with an inline margin override. Incidents uses a bare .has-help with the subtitle outside it. Others use neither. The result is that the top of every screen sits at a slightly different height, which is the single loudest signal that a product was built screen by screen.

The contract

PageHeader takes title, optional subtitle, optional helpHref, optional actions, optional status. It owns its own bottom margin so no caller writes an inline one.

Actions, not decoration

At most one filled button per header, always the thing the screen exists to do. Exports and print stay secondary. PageActions keeps its existing argument about links versus buttons and slots into actions.

Status strip

The "what is outstanding" summary Incidents already computes moves out of an ad-hoc .card and into the header's own strip. Same data, same rule — count what is unresolved, never what happened.

Health flags sit on the row

An allergy or a medication due is only useful at the moment somebody is signing that child in or out. listHealthByChild is already fetched — render it as a warn-tone flag beside the name rather than leaving it to the child record.

Queue state is visible, not implied

The outbox already merges into the client roll. Say so: a pending-tone count on the section heading, and a small pending dot on the individual rows that came from the queue.

Sign-out targets stay at 44px

The 32px in-row button is a reasonable desk compromise elsewhere. Not here — this row is tapped on a tablet in a doorway, so it takes --target-min.

Flags before facts

Allergies, medication and anything overdue sit directly under the name in breach and warn tones. Everything else — dates, room, enrolment — is reference material and reads as such.

Tabs are routes, not state

Use /children/[id]/[tab] so a manager can link a colleague straight to the health tab. A tab held in useState cannot be sent to anybody.

The parent view is the same page

A guardian already reaches this record through a policy that scopes it to their own child. Do not build a second component — hide the tabs their capabilities cannot fill and let the page be one page.

07 — Incident form

The form filled in under pressure

An incident report is written by someone who has just dealt with an incident, often on a tablet, often with a child nearby. It is also a legal record that cannot be edited once final. Those two facts pull in opposite directions, and the layout has to hold both: fast to complete, impossible to submit by accident.

New report

Save a draft at any point. Nothing is sent to whānau until you finalise, and a finalised report can only be corrected by an amendment that keeps the original.

Two buttons, two consequences

Save draft and Finalise never look alike and never sit adjacent without the warning between them. The existing edit-versus-amend rule already refuses the wrong operation server-side; the layout should make it obvious before the tap.

The action bar sticks

On a tablet the description field pushes the buttons below the fold, and a half-written report abandoned because the writer could not find Save is the failure mode this screen has. Pin the bar to the bottom of the viewport while the form is open.

Amend states its lineage

When ?amend= opens the form, the left column says which report is being corrected and what the original said. An amendment written without the original in view is how two records disagree.

The header counts what is wrong

"9 people" is inventory. "2 certificates expiring" is the reason somebody opened the screen. Every list header carries the outstanding count beside the total, using the same rule the incident register already follows.

Settings is sections, not a wall

Group the centre settings into Centre details, Hours and rooms, Notifications, Integrations, Data and retention — each a card with its own save. A single form with one save button at the bottom of forty fields is a form nobody finishes.

Accounts shows the ledger, not the total

A family balance means nothing without the last three movements against it. Every account row expands to its recent transactions in place rather than navigating away.

09 — Kiosk

A door tablet is not a web page

This route already has its own stylesheet and its own reasoning about what it deliberately omits. It should also have its own scale: one column, targets at --target-primary, type at the mobile base and above, and no affordance that leads anywhere else.

Say the connection out loud

There is no offline queue here, on purpose. So the screen needs a persistent connection strip — quiet when online, breach-tone and blocking when not — rather than letting a tap fail at the moment a parent is standing there.

One outcome per screen

Pick a child → confirm the adult → enter the PIN → a full-screen confirmation that clears itself after eight seconds. No back link, no partial state left on the tablet for the next person in the queue.

PIN entry is its own layout

A numeric keypad drawn in the page, not a text input that summons the OS keyboard over the roll. Digits masked, four --target-primary rows, one clear affordance.

10 — Mobile

Leave the tab bars alone

Four labelled tabs, roll first for staff, tamariki first for whānau, no icons. That structure is right and the reasoning in the navigators holds. The work on mobile is inside the screens: the card, the empty state and the offline strip.

The offline strip earns a fixed slot

OfflineStrip currently appears and disappears, which reflows the roll under a thumb mid-tap. Reserve its height always: empty and silent when the queue is clear, pending-tone with a count when it is not.

One sign-in target, not a row of them

ChildCard should carry a single 44px action whose label is the next state — "Sign in" or "Sign out", never both — with the flag row above it. Two adjacent buttons at arm's length is a mis-tap that changes a legal record.

Empty states say what happens next

EmptyState exists — give it a required action prop and use it on Pānui, Messages and Tamariki. "No posts yet" is a dead end; "Nothing from the centre yet — you'll get a notification" is an answer.
